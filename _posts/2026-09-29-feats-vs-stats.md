So, you've hopped your small pond for a big one and found that there are people out there orders of magnitude better than you at your craft. You've been brutally IQmogged, creativitymogged and effortmogged by people who do the exact thing you spend the majority of your time, attention and energy on. Questions: how are you going to cope? Is there any hope for you? What do you have in common with anime powerscalers?

Let's start with the last one. For those not in the know, powerscaling is the hobby of trying to work out which fictional characters would win in fights against each other, particularly characters from different pieces of media whom we would never get to see interact. Would Gojo from Jujutsu Kaisen beat One Punch Man from, well, One Punch Man? Would the Roman empire prevail if invaded from the north by the armies of Mordor? (Sauron does not have his ring). Who would win in a swordfight, Jaime Lannister (full strength) or Musashi Miyamoto?

There are, to my knowledge, two different schools of thought on how to sensibly make these comparisons. The first would compare characters on their abilities, which we'll call their "stats". Who has higher "battle IQ"? (tactical intuition and strategic situational awareness) Sheer strength? General intelligence and creativity? Charisma, as is pertinent to the ability to rally allies? Endurance under hardship? The idea is to tally up who wins on each front and then based on the ratio of strengths determine whether one character would "neg diff" the other (destroy them with such ease that it would take a negative amount of effort), just about prevail, or in rare cases conclude that the characters are equally matched.

This is the way I, and a lot of people I know, tend to compare themselves with others. In my field - maths - I might see whether someone knows more esoteric theory, has stronger geometric intuition, stronger physics intuition, better discrete maths/combinatorics problem solving ability, better number theoretic intuition, more conscientiousness about rigour, more irritation at an unsolved problem in their mind. To my dismay, I've met people who currently no diff me or even neg diff me.

In fact, nowadays, even those at the pinnacle of mathematics are experiencing [deep blue](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/15/deep-blue/), as artificial intelligence has grown better than even the strongest mathematicians at persevering through difficulty and solving the most important open problems, a skill which requires considerable creativity, patience, and panache. [Mathematicians have had various ways of coping with this](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3367197/ill-write-romance-novels-if-ai-solves-all-maths-chinese-fields-winner-jokes), but it is undeniable that these developments have been hard on the self-esteem of those with any level of competitive drive.

There is an alternative way of looking at things, which doesn't send your ego into crisis mode but also isn't just cope that leads you to complacency. This is the second school of thought of powerscaling. Compare characters based on their feats. Rather than estimating their stats, compare them on their actual achievements. Would Thorfinn Karlsefni (as characterised in Vinland Saga), prevail against Oberyn Martell? Martell may have had more formal training and raw skill with his weapon, but Thorfinn managed to take down Thorkell the Tall in a duel, and to hold his own against Askeladd at age 14 (till his anger got the better of him). Despite his shortness and his homegrown dagger wielding style, I would put Thorfinn in with a strong fighting chance. (although to be true to the characters, they'd be more likely to be friends than opponents)

Who are the greatest physicists of history? Surely, the originators of quantum mechanics - Schrodinger, Bohr, etc. - deserve to throw their hats in the ring, despite the 1920s being a time when "second rate physicists did first rate physics". Dirac meant it as an insult, but to me, it is inspiring. Regardless of your raw processing rates, can you use the whole of your person, your perspicacity, your savviness at choosing problems to work on, your pattern of attention (be it wide and scattered, gaining many disparate influences, or deep and beamlike, penetrating far into one field), to do great and original work?

Of course, all else being equal, it would be better to have more of any given stat. And to the extent that it is within your control to improve your stats, to gain stronger intuitions, to become more fluent and competent, it is certainly worth it. But only instrumentally, not as an end in and of itself. Chasing stats as your purpose leads to strange and pathological places, like making empty boasts about your membership in smart people clubs like Mensa, instead of focusing your mental energy on something more worthwhile.

Here's a question better than any of the three we started with that I'd like to keep in my mind: what kind of crazy feats can I accomplish?
