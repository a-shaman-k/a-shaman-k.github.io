So, you've hopped your small pond for a big one and found that there are people out there orders of magnitude better than you at your craft. You've been brutally IQmogged, creativitymogged and effortmogged by people who do the exact thing you spend the majority of your time, attention and energy on. Questions: how are you going to cope? Is there any hope for you? What do you have in common with anime powerscalers?

Let's start with the last one. For those not in the know, powerscaling is the hobby of trying to work out which fictional characters would win in fights against each other, particularly characters from different pieces of media whom we would never get to see interact. Would Gojo from Jujutsu Kaisen beat One Punch Man from, well, One Punch Man? Would the Roman empire prevail if invaded from the north by the armies of Mordor? (Sauron does not have his ring). Who would win in a sword fight, Jaime Lannister (full strength) or Musashi Miyamoto?

There are, to my knowledge, two different schools of thought on how to sensibly make these comparisons. The first would compare characters on their abilities, which we'll call their "stats". Who has higher "battle IQ"? (tactical intuition and strategic situational awareness) Who has more sheer strength? What about general intelligence and creativity? Endurance under hardship? The idea is to tally up who wins on each front and then based on the ratio of tallies determine which character would win. 

Potential outcomes are:
-they draw, so are evenly matched and fights could go either way depending on circumstance, preparation, and dumb luck
-one of them "mid diffs", so one character is clearly stronger, but still faces a moderate amount of difficulty
-one character "no diffs" the other, i.e. they destroy the other with no difficulty
-one character "neg diffs" the other. They are even more mismatched in ability than the no diff case, such that the stronger character faces negative difficulty

This is similar to the way I, and a lot of people I know, tend to compare themselves with others. In my field - maths - I might see whether someone knows more esoteric theory, has stronger geometric intuition, stronger physics intuition, better discrete maths/combinatorics problem solving ability, better number theoretic intuition, more conscientiousness about rigour, faces more irritation than me at an unsolved problem in their mind. To my dismay, I've been meeting people who currently no diff me or even neg diff me along these lines.

In fact, nowadays, even those at the pinnacle of mathematics are experiencing [deep blue](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/15/deep-blue/), as artificial intelligence has grown better than even the strongest mathematicians at persevering through difficulty and solving the most important open problems, a skill which requires considerable creativity, patience, and panache. [Mathematicians have had various ways of coping with this](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3367197/ill-write-romance-novels-if-ai-solves-all-maths-chinese-fields-winner-jokes), but it is undeniable that these developments have been hard on the self-esteem of those with any level of competitive drive.

There is an alternative way of looking at things, which doesn't send your ego into crisis mode but also isn't just cope that leads you to complacency. This is the second school of thought of powerscaling. Compare characters based on their feats. Rather than estimating their stats, compare them on their actual achievements. Would Thorfinn Karlsefni (as characterised in Vinland Saga), prevail against Oberyn Martell (Game of Thrones)? Martell may win on stats and training, but Thorfinn managed to take down Thorkell the Tall in a duel, and to hold his own against Askeladd at age 14 (till his anger got the better of him). Despite his shortness and his homegrown dagger wielding style, I would put Thorfinn in with a strong fighting chance after taking these feats into account. (although to be true to the characters, they'd be more likely to be friends than opponents)

Who are the greatest physicists of history? Surely, the originators of quantum mechanics - Schrodinger, Bohr, etc. - deserve to throw their hats in the ring, despite the 1920s being a time when "second rate physicists did first rate work". Dirac meant it as an insult, but to me, it is inspiring. Regardless of your raw mental processing rate, can you use the whole of your person, all of your traits: your perspicacity, your savviness at choosing problems to work on, your pattern of attention (be it wide and scattered, gaining many disparate influences, or deep and beamlike, penetrating far into one field), to do great and original work?

Of course, all else being equal, it would be better to have more of any given stat. And to the extent that it is within your control to improve your stats, to gain stronger intuitions, to become more fluent and competent, it is certainly worth it. But only instrumentally, not as an end in and of itself. Chasing better stats as your final purpose leads to strange and pathological places, like making empty boasts about your membership in smart-people-clubs like Mensa, instead of focusing your mental energy on something more worthwhile.

Here's a question better than any of the three we started with, one that I'd like to keep in my mind: what kind of crazy feats can I accomplish?
